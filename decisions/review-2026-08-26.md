## 3. The three questions you actually asked

### AVGO — the cleanest head-to-head in the file

I proposed a $152 TRIM at $380.00 on a sourced catalyst_threat (BofA's XPV financing downgrade, reinforced by the WSJ's $3T off-balance-sheet financing report). You doubled the position, 2sh → 4sh, the same day at a qty-weighted $364.605.

AVGO is now $351.195. That's **-7.58% from my anchor and -3.68% from your entry.** Both of us watched it go the same direction. I said sell; you bought more; the stock fell either way.

**I was right, you were wrong, and it cost something concrete: -$26.82 realized-on-paper on the 2sh you added.** The catalyst thesis (financing-structure scrutiny on hyperscaler capex vehicles) is playing out exactly as sourced. This isn't a coin-flip call — it's the one where the reasoning and the price both lined up against the trade you made.

### NBIS — stay-out vs. one-day re-entry

I advised staying out of SKHY/NBIS/IONQ re-entry this week. You re-entered NBIS one day after being stopped out at $259.90, buying 4sh at $217.88.

Two things are true at once here, and I won't collapse them into one number:

- **My directional call was right.** From where I was looking at it (~$228.50 on 08-19), NBIS is now $215.48 — down another 5.70%. If you'd genuinely stayed flat from that point, you'd have avoided a real decline, past the neutral band.
- **But your actual entry price was better than my observation point** — you caught it after it had already fallen further intraday. From your $217.88 fill, NBIS is down only -1.10% ($9.60 on 4sh) — inside the neutral band, a wash, not a loss.

So: the direction I called was validated: NBIS kept falling. But the damage from re-entering was minimal because your timing inside the day was good. **This isn't "the desk beat the user" or "the user beat the desk" — it's the desk being right about the stock and the user being lucky-to-skilled about the fill.** The original stop that got you out at $259.90, meanwhile, is one of the best performers in Cohort A: +17.09%, the single largest dollar contributor in the whole file.

### BE and COHR — did the cap protect you, or cost you?

I flagged BE at negative ATR headroom (-$88.54, already 1.118x over cap) and COHR near-full ($69.91 headroom, 0.929x cap). You added to both anyway: BE +4sh at $201.635, COHR +2sh at $285.61.

**Both trades made money.** BE is +6.02% (+$48.56), COHR is +1.61% (+$9.22, inside the neutral band but on the right side of it). At one week, **the cap would have cost you money on BE and been roughly neutral on COHR** — the ATR ceiling was, this week, too conservative rather than protective.

I want to be precise about what that does and doesn't prove. One green week on an over-cap add is not evidence the cap is wrong — ATR caps exist to bound tail risk on names that have already moved a lot, and BE in particular is a high-beta name where a +6% week can flip to a -6% week without warning. What it does prove is that the cap didn't fire because the stock was about to fall — this time. File it as a data point, not a rule change.
