How to read this / why it exists
Until 2026-08-12 essentially every proposal this desk produced was an ATR risk-cap trim. Five compounding causes: the scorer weighted over_cap highest and penalised a buy with no breach; the relative-strength cache was 12 days stale with a benchmark return 18.6pp wrong, starving the only profit-take trigger; per-name RSI did not exist at all, so OVERBOUGHT PULLBACK had fired once in 69 journal entries; the book ran on its two weakest signals while OVERSOLD BOUNCE (100% interim 7d, n=4) sat dormant; and repeat_count promoted proposals that had in effect been declined.

LIVE triggers may be sized into proposals now. SHADOW triggers are logged to trigger_journal.json with a flag price and scored at 7/30d first — they contribute zero to proposal priority until they earn a measured hit rate, the same rule the de-risk queue runs under.

Overbought→trim is deliberately cap-independent. A name comfortably inside its risk cap is still a valid profit-take; gating profit-taking on a breach is exactly what made every trim an ATR trim.
Thresholds use hysteresis — oversold fires below 35 and retires above 50, overbought fires above 70 and retires below 60 — so a name hovering at a threshold does not flip between open and retired on noise.
The quality gate for a dip-buy is the thesis, not the signal. Requiring net-bullish signals would disqualify every oversold name by definition. Only a fundamental negative disqualifies.

How this is scored
Fragility — share of the book’s total open risk (already embeds ATR × position size) multiplied by how far the position sits over its own 2×ATR cap. This is how hard it hits, and it is the dominant term.
Stretch — 1-month return relative to SMH, but only counted when the name is also up in absolute terms. Beating a benchmark that is itself falling means the name merely fell less; there is no gain to give back, so it is not a trim candidate. Relative rather than absolute deliberately: in a ~100% single-factor book an absolute RSI/52-week screen flags all-or-nothing.
Friction — cost of acting: proximity to the 24-month LTCG boundary (from lots.json), unknown lot dates, and a dust-position discount. Higher friction pushes a name down the queue.
Sentiment is an urgency dial on the whole queue (band extreme_greed → ×1.25), never a trigger. It cannot manufacture stretch that does not exist per name.

This queue is shadow-scored and does not drive proposals. Each deep run logs its top names to derisk_journal.json and scores them at 30/90d. It earns a vote in sizing decisions only once it has a real hit rate.
